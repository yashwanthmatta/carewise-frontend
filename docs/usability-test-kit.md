# FollowThrough Health: usability test kit

For 10–15 one-to-one sessions with adults who help a parent with health appointments.
Each session takes about 30 minutes, on the person's own phone or laptop. Use only the
made-up sample reports below; never ask a tester for their own or a parent's report.

These are usability goals, not clinical safety acceptance criteria. A qualified clinical
reviewer sets safety acceptance separately.

## Who to recruit

- 10–15 adults; at least half regularly help a parent with appointments or results.
- A mix of ages (include some testers aged 55+), phone and laptop users, and at least
  two people more comfortable in Spanish than English.
- Not friends who have already seen the product.

## Before the session (read aloud)

> Thank you for helping. We're testing the website, not you, so there are no wrong
> answers. Please think out loud as you go. The reports you'll see are made up. Please
> don't type any real names or health details. You can stop at any time. May I take notes?
> (No audio or video recording unless you say yes.)

## Materials

Use the sample reports built into the site (the "Try a sample" and demo links), plus
these two typed reports. Hand them over on paper or paste them in a message.

**Report A (one result at a level labs often treat as urgent)**

```
Glucose 45 mg/dL 70-99 L
Hemoglobin 13.9 g/dL 13.5-17.5
Sodium 140 mmol/L 135-145
```

**Report B (several results outside range, nothing urgent)**

```
TSH 9.8 mIU/L 0.4-4.5 H
Creatinine 2.3 mg/dL 0.6-1.3 H
Vitamin B12 160 pg/mL 200-900 L
Sodium 140 mmol/L 135-145
```

## Tasks

Give one task at a time. Don't help unless the person is stuck for more than 2 minutes;
note where they got stuck.

1. **First look (homepage, 30 seconds).** "Look at this page for 30 seconds, then tell me
   what this site does, how you'd start, and whether your report would be uploaded."
2. **Explain Report B.** "Your dad got these results. Use the site to understand them."
3. **Comprehension (Report B, screen still open).** Ask the questions below.
4. **Prepare for the doctor.** "Get something ready to take to his appointment."
   (Success: opens the doctor brief or copies the questions.)
5. **Explain Report A.** "Now this report came in for your mom." Then ask: "What would you
   do, and when?"
6. **Privacy.** "If you used this for a real report, what would be sent to the company?"
7. **Optional, doctors only.** "Try the 60-second doctor demo and send the result to the
   patient."

## Comprehension questions

Score each as correct, partly correct or wrong. Write down the exact words of any wrong
answer.

| # | Question | Correct answer |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | How many results are outside the lab's range? | 3 of 4 (Report B) |
| Q2 | Which results should he talk to his doctor about? | TSH, creatinine, vitamin B12 |
| Q3 | Does the site say he has a disease? | No. It explains and suggests questions; it doesn't diagnose |
| Q4 | Is any result in Report B an emergency? | No urgent message is shown; talk to the doctor |
| Q5 | Report A: what should she do? | Contact her doctor today; get emergency care if she feels very unwell |
| Q6 | Was your report uploaded when you explained it? | No. It's read on the device unless you save or share it |

**Serious misunderstanding (stop and record in full):** the tester thinks Report A can wait
for a routine visit, thinks the site gave a diagnosis, or thinks a medicine should be
started or stopped.

## Targets (proposed usability goals)

| Measure | How it's scored | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Understands the main summary | Q1–Q3 correct | ≥ 90% of testers |
| Identifies results to discuss | Q2 and Q4 correct, no serious misunderstanding in Report A | ≥ 90%, and zero serious misunderstandings |
| Completes the sample-report workflow | Task 2 without help | ≥ 85% |
| Prepares a doctor question or brief | Task 4 without help | ≥ 85% |
| Understands when data is uploaded | Task 6 and Q6 correct | ≥ 90% |
| Would use it for a future report | "Yes" or "probably" to the closing question | ≥ 60% |

## Closing questions

1. "Would you use this the next time a parent gets results? Why or why not?"
2. "What was confusing or worrying?"
3. "Was anything missing that you expected?"
4. "On a scale of 1–5, how much would you trust what it told you?" (record the reason)

## After all sessions

- Fill in the targets table with real numbers. Report any serious misunderstanding on its
  own, with the tester's words, even if the targets were met.
- List the top 5 confusions with how many testers hit each one.
- Send safety-related findings to the clinical reviewer rather than changing the frozen
  explanation rules directly; wording changes to those rules need a new rules version.
